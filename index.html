Slide 2: The OTIS Analytical Framework

To analyze literature effectively, we use four clear steps to guide our thinking:

O Observe

Look at the raw evidence. What specific words, imagery, or details stand out on the page?

T Trace

Consider the context. Who wrote or spoke this? What background situation surrounds it?

I Interpret

Uncover deeper meaning. What message or theme is the author conveying beneath the surface?

S Synthesize

Connect it all. How does this passage connect to our bigger questions about American Literature?

Slide 3: Warm-Up Exercise — Close Reading

Let's practice the Observe and Trace steps with this poem:

Cause I Ain't Got a Pencil

by Joshua T. Dickerson

I woke myself up Because we ain't got an alarm clock Dug in the dirty clothes basket, Cause ain't nobody washed my uniform Brushed my hair and teeth in the dark, Cause the lights ain't on Even got my baby sister ready, Cause my mama wasn't home. Got us both to school on time, To eat us a good breakfast. Then when I got to class the teacher fussed Cause I ain't got a pencil.
OTIS Activity Prompt:

List 3 specific details from the speaker's morning routine. How does the final line change our understanding of why the student didn't have a pencil?

Slide 4: Origin Stories & Oral Traditions

American Literature begins long before printed books, rooted in rich Indigenous oral traditions.

Key Concept: Cosmogonic Myth

Cosmogonic Myth (noun) — A traditional narrative explaining how the world, nature, and human society were created.

In our next reading, "Manabozho and the Muskrat" (Ojibwe Tradition), we will observe how different cultures celebrate bravery, humility, and community cooperation through storytelling.
