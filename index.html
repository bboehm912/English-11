2. Indigenous Oral Traditions & Storytelling

MN ELA 11.1.1.1
  • Orality vs. Literacy: Indigenous narratives rely on oral performance, mnemonic structures, and communal repetition rather than static written texts.
  • Cultural Continuity: Stories served as living histories, legal codes, spiritual guides, and environmental instruction manuals.
  • Fluid Performance: Each telling adapts dynamically to the audience while maintaining core cultural truths.

3. Cosmogony & Creation Myths

MN ELA 11.1.3.3
  • Definition: Cosmogony explores narratives explaining the origin and structure of the universe.
  • Earth-Diver Motifs: Common in Native American literature, where animals dive into primordial waters to retrieve mud and form land.
  • Ecological Balance: Humans are viewed as equal partners in creation rather than dominant rulers over nature.

4. Comparative Origin Stories

MN ELA 11.1.9.9
  • Iroquois vs. Genesis: Contrast "The World on Turtle's Back" with Judeo-Christian creation traditions.
  • Dualistic Balance: Exploring the twin brothers motif (right-handed vs. left-handed twin) as necessary harmony rather than simple good vs. evil.
  • Textual Evidence: Analyzing how worldview shapes literary archetype and character motivations.

5. Colonial Encounters & Early American Texts

MN ELA 11.1.2.2
  • First-Contact Narratives: Examining Cabeza de Vaca, Bradford, and Rowlandson through critical historical lenses.
  • Perspective & Bias: Dissecting early European journals for rhetorical positioning and ideological bias.
  • Captivity Narratives: Analyzing literary tropes and popular propaganda in early colonial publishing.

6. Rhetoric of Revolution & Independence

MN ELA 11.2.4.4
  • Founding Documents: Ethos, Pathos, and Logos in Patrick Henry, Thomas Paine, and Thomas Jefferson.
  • Persuasive Appeals: How revolutionary pamphlets mobilized public sentiment across colonies.
  • Literary Paradox: Examining liberty rhetoric alongside the persistence of slavery in early America.

7. Transcendentalism & The American Wild

MN ELA 11.1.4.4
  • Emerson & Thoreau: Self-Reliance, Over-Soul, and civil disobedience in 19th-century thought.
  • Nature as Sanctuary: Rejecting industrial materialism in favor of individual spiritual intuition.
  • Modern Resonance: Connecting 1840s Transcendentalism to modern environmental ethics.

8. Media Literacy & Cultural Narrative Analysis

MN ELA 11.3.1.1
  • Media as Mythmakers: How modern films, news outlets, and digital media construct national mythologies.
  • Deconstructing Tropes: Identifying recurring cultural archetypes across print and visual media formats.
  • Critical Lens: Questioning who holds narrative power and whose voices remain marginalized.

9. The O.T.I.S. Analytical Framework

Core Course Methodology
  • O - Objectivity: Distinguish verifiable textual facts from reader assumption and emotional bias.
  • T - Truthfulness: Evaluate authorial intent, logical consistency, and historical contextual accuracy.
  • I - Integrity: Execute rigorous primary source citation and ethical scholarly synthesis.
  • S - Stewardship: Respect diverse cultural traditions and maintain responsible classroom dialogue.

10. Critical Reading & Textual Annotation

MN ELA 11.1.1.2
  • Active Annotation: Coding texts for rhetorical devices, thematic motifs, and structural shifts.
  • Margin Dialogue: Writing analytical questions and connections directly alongside primary source passages.
  • Vocabulary in Context: Tracking archaic diction and shift in word meanings across literary eras.

11. Textual Evidence & MLA Citation Standards

MN ELA 11.2.8.8
  • Signal Phrases: Integrating direct quotes seamlessly into analytical sentences.
  • Parenthetical Citations: Formatting MLA in-text citations accurately for print and digital sources.
  • Scholarship Ethics: Avoiding plagiarism through precise attribution and original paraphrase.

12. Synthesizing Multiple Media Formats

MN ELA 11.3.2.2
  • Cross-Media Analysis: Pairing 19th-century essays with 21st-century documentary media.
  • Comparative Matrices: Charting thematic alignment across written, visual, and audio texts.
  • Synthesized Claims: Formulating complex thesis statements that evaluate multiple mediums simultaneously.

13. Course Expectations & Discussion Ethics

Classroom Standards
  • Socratic Seminar Norms: Speak with evidence, listen actively, and challenge ideas rather than people.
  • Preparation Mandate: Arrive with completed readings, marked texts, and written discussion questions.
  • Academic Rigor: Revision opportunities require full reflection submissions within one week of feedback.
